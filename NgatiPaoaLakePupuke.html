Waitemata Harbour

Waitematā Harbour is Aucklands main access by sea and is often called Auckland Harbour, though it is one of two harbours adjoining the city. It forms the northern and eastern coasts of the Auckland isthmus and is crossed by the Auckland Harbour Bridge. On the southern side, it is matched by the shallower Manukau Harbour. Covering 70 square miles (180 km²), it connects the city’s main port and waterfront to the Hauraki Gulf and the Pacific Ocean, sheltered from storms by Aucklands North Shore, Rangitoto Island, and Waiheke Island.

Rangitoto Island

Rangitoto Island is a volcanic island in the Hauraki Gulf. The 5.5 km (3.4 mi) wide island is a symmetrical shield volcano, reaching 260 m (850 ft) in height. It is the youngest and largest of Auckland’s ~50 volcanoes, formed about 600 years ago, covering 2,311 ha (5,710 acres). Separated from Auckland’s North Shore by the Rangitoto Channel, it has been linked by a causeway to the older, non-volcanic Motutapu Island since World War II.
